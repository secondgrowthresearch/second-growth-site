# 250 jobs, measured, not modeled

Most jobs figures in economic research are multipliers applied to a dollar amount — a real method, but an inference. This page documents a different kind of figure: a real, named, counted headcount, at a real site inside this project's own four counties.

## Dos Rios: a real, counted jobs figure

Dos Rios Ranch Preserve — now Dos Rios State Park, at the Tuolumne/San Joaquin river confluence in Stanislaus County — is River Partners' flagship floodplain-restoration project. Its own reporting names 250 local jobs created over the site's build-out, against $40 million invested from 11 separate funding sources over roughly a decade. That $40M/250-jobs figure describes a program total spread over ten years, not an annual rate — it isn't directly comparable to a one-year construction multiplier, and we're not dividing it into one. Dos Rios's own acreage is reported inconsistently across sources we've checked — 1,600, 2,100, and "nearly 2,400" acres each appear in different River Partners materials — logged here rather than resolved to one, since no single authoritative figure was found.

## A labor pipeline already operating

The Greater Valley Conservation Corps, a California Conservation Corps-model youth corps, has worked at Dos Rios removing invasive plants and clearing river debris along the San Joaquin River. This is a real, current example of restoration labor and this region's displaced-industry workforce operating in the same place — just at youth-corps scale, not full-career adult employment. A second, larger California Wildlife Conservation Board grant ($40 million, awarded September 2023) aims to replicate the Dos Rios model across 6,000+ acres in Merced County and beyond; no jobs figure has been reported for it yet.

## What habitat land costs to maintain, year after year

A Center for Natural Lands Management study prepared for the EPA (2004) surveyed 28 conservation preserves, nine in California, and found average annual management costs of $51/acre/year (median $122/acre/year, full range $6 to over $2,100/acre/year depending on the site). That's a real, if dated, per-acre O&M literature for habitat land specifically — and it's one this project could not find an equivalent for in engineered recharge-basin infrastructure, despite a dedicated search. That gap is itself worth noting: the published cost-accounting record for habitat/restoration land stewardship is more mature than for engineered recharge basins, at least based on what this research could locate.

USDA NRCS's Agricultural Conservation Easement Program (Wetland Reserve Easements component) is real and well-funded — over $4 billion nationally through FY2031 under 2025 reconciliation legislation, with NRCS paying 75% of restoration costs on a typical 30-year or permanent easement. The Army Corps of Engineers' Section 206 authority can also fund aquatic-ecosystem restoration directly, but at much smaller scale (roughly $8–11 million/year nationally) and only once a local public agency or nonprofit sponsor formally requests a feasibility study — it doesn't start on its own.

## What we don't know

- Dos Rios's actual acreage, unresolved across three different figures in River Partners' own materials.
- Whether the second, $40M replication grant has produced a jobs figure yet — not found as of this research.
- A per-acre breakdown for any of the nine California preserves in the 2004 CNLM/EPA study, which would sharpen the $51–122/acre/year range into a figure specific to this region's land type.
