# Del Monte closes its Hanford tomato plant — Kings County, 2026

In March 2026, Del Monte closed its Hanford tomato-processing plant — the only tomato-processing facility in the company's entire ten-plant U.S./Mexico roster — eliminating 378 to 500-plus jobs.

## What happened

Food manufacturing wasn't a marginal part of Kings County's economy when this plant closed. By our [Sector Dependence Index](../the-index.md), the sector's share of the county's entire export-driven economic base **more than doubled between 1990 and 2025** against the U.S. benchmark — meaning the closure landed on a *growing*, increasingly central pillar of the county's economy, not a shrinking, marginal one. That's a measurable claim, not an impression — see [the Index](../the-index.md) for the full numbers and [Methodology](../methodology.md) for how we calculated them.

Del Monte Foods filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in July 2025, months before this closure — see the timeline on the main [Case Studies](../case-studies.md) page for how that filing relates to both this closure and the company's larger Modesto/Hughson closure the following month.

## Impact — what we know, and what we don't

- **Jobs:** 378 confirmed by WARN filing; press coverage cites figures above 500 for the fuller count across phased layoffs.
- **What happened to the workers:** Not yet documented — this archive has not found reemployment or workforce-outcome data for the Hanford closure specifically.
- **County-level trend:** Kings County's dependence on food-processing was *rising*, not falling, when this closure hit — the opposite trajectory from Stanislaus County's [Modesto/Hughson closure](stanislaus-2026-del-monte-modesto-hughson.md), which landed on a *declining* share. We haven't yet explained why these two flagship closures sit on opposite economic trajectories; it's a real open question for further research.

## Part of a larger pattern

Hanford is one of two Kings County towns — along with Lemoore — that have now absorbed multiple commodity-processing closures: [Olam's 2020 Lemoore tomato-plant closure](kings-2020-olam-lemoore.md) and [Leprino Foods' 2025–26 Lemoore East dairy-plant closure](kings-2025-leprino-lemoore.md). This is the clearest documented case of the pattern this project tracks: not an isolated plant closing, but the same small county absorbing multiple, sequential single-industry exits.
