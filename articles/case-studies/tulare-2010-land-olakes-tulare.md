# Land O'Lakes closes its Tulare cheese plant — Tulare County, 2010

Land O'Lakes idled the cheese-manufacturing line at its Tulare plant in 2010, cutting about 80 jobs — the earliest commodity-processing closure this project has documented in Tulare County, and the first of four such closures found across the Central Valley's four-county record for the 2010–2016 window, more than a decade before the 2024–2026 wave this project otherwise tracks.

## What happened

Land O'Lakes, the dairy cooperative, announced on April 27, 2010 that it would close the cheese-manufacturing operation at its Tulare site, with the shutdown expected in the second half of 2010. A secondary WARN tracker lists a notice date of August 14, 2010, covering 80 employees — this archive could not independently verify that filing against the official state WARN report, which does not cover a period this far back.

The company tied the decision to declining California milk volumes and to a recent investment it had made in the same site's *butter*-manufacturing operations — shifting milk there instead of to cheese production was, in the company's framing, the more efficient choice. A company executive called it "a difficult decision that was driven by a variety of factors." Affected employees were offered the company's Employee Assistance Program, and Land O'Lakes said it would work with the Teamsters Union on the transition.

The Tulare site's butter-manufacturing operation was not affected and kept running — this was a partial exit, one product line closing while the rest of the site continued, the same pattern this project later documents at Foster Farms' Turlock plant in 2025.

## Impact — what we know, and what we don't

- **Jobs:** ~80, per the company's own figure and a secondary WARN tracker; not independently confirmed against an official state filing.
- **What happened to the workers:** Beyond the Employee Assistance Program offer, no source found tracks outcomes.
- **The building's fate:** No source found describes what became of the cheese-manufacturing building or equipment after 2010.
- **A discrepancy in the reporting itself:** coverage of this closure uses both "idle" and "close" to describe it, sometimes in the same outlet — not resolved by the source text.

## Part of a larger pattern

This project's main record documents commodity-processing closures from 2020 forward. Searching the 2000–2020 window specifically, to see whether the pattern reaches back further in the Central Valley's four core counties, turned up four: this one (2010), [Dawn Foods' Modesto bakeries](stanislaus-2012-dawn-foods-modesto.md) (March 2012), [Patterson Vegetable Company](stanislaus-2012-patterson-vegetable.md) (June 2012), and [ConAgra's Hunt-Wesson plant in Helm](fresno-2016-conagra-helm.md) (2016). None of these were previously documented anywhere in this project's record. Tulare County, in particular, had no documented closure case study before this one — the county's continuing exposure to the same sector (noted on the main [Case Studies](../case-studies.md) page) now has an actual historical anchor point.
