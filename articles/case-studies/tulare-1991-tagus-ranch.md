# Tagus Ranch's raisin plant closes near Tulare — Tulare County, 1991

Tagus Ranch Packing Company's raisin-packing plant closed on March 31, 1991, laying off 60 workers — the quiet last act of a once-world-class fruit empire, and a rare case in this project where a highway, not an agricultural market, is the named cause of decline.

## What happened

Tagus Ranch was founded in 1912 by Hulett C. Merritt, who came west to build a second fortune after striking it rich in U.S. Steel. At its height, the ranch grew peaches, apricots, prunes, boysenberries, and cotton, housing its field-worker crews in twelve wood-and-adobe camps spread across the property — and it was more than a farm: a restaurant and hotel made it "a lively stop for travelers" on Highway 99. The ranch's fortunes declined as more restaurants opened in Visalia and Tulare and, decisively, as Interstate 5 diverted north-south traffic away from Highway 99 — an infrastructure cause, not a market one, that's distinct from the agricultural-economics explanations behind most of this project's other closures. By 1991, the raisin-packing plant was apparently the last significant piece of the once-sprawling operation still running. The plant had been for sale for a couple of months before the closure; company officials declined to give a specific reason beyond "a lot of different reasons," and no union represented its 60 employees.

## Impact — what we know, and what we don't

- **Jobs:** 60, per the Tulare Advance-Register's contemporaneous front-page coverage.
- **Whether the plant reopened under new ownership:** not documented in any source found.
- **What became of the broader Tagus Ranch property — the restaurant, hotel, and worker camps:** not documented in any source found.

## Part of a larger pattern

This is the earliest commercial closure documented anywhere in Tulare County's record in this project, a year before [Kraft's Louis Rich turkey plant](tulare-1992-kraft-louis-rich-tulare.md) closed nearby — meaning the county's two oldest documented closures land within a year of each other, matching the "compounding closures" shape found independently in Kings and Stanislaus counties for the same general period. In scale and arc, Tagus Ranch's eight-decade run from a spectacular founding to a quiet exit echoes [Lacey Milling Co.'s](../case-studies/kings-2023-lacey-milling-hanford.md) 136-year run in Hanford — far smaller at its peak, but the same shape of a long-running local institution eventually closing out.
