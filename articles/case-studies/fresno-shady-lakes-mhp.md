# A mobile-home park, a lawsuit, and a $200,000 settlement — Shady Lakes, Fresno County

Shady Lakes Mobile Home Park, home mostly to Indigenous Mexican farmworker families near Fresno, is this project's clearest example of a water-and-housing court case actually reaching a resolution — residents sued and won a real settlement, not just a promised fix.

## What happened

Shady Lakes Mobile Home Park sits within the Washington Irrigated Colony disadvantaged unincorporated community near Fresno, home mostly to Indigenous Mexican farmworker families. California Rural Legal Assistance brought a lawsuit against Harmony, the park's owner, on residents' behalf. As of CRLA's December 2023 litigation update, the case had settled: residents secured a $200,000 payment along with commitments from Harmony regarding conditions at the park. The update does not specify the underlying conditions at issue — our sourcing confirms the settlement and the amount, not the full list of grievances that led to it.

## Impact — what we know, and what we don't

- **What specifically residents sued over:** Not specified in the source reviewed — CRLA's litigation summary confirms the settlement and payment but not the underlying conditions (water, housing conditions, or both).
- **Whether the commitments Harmony made have been fulfilled:** Not independently confirmed past the settlement itself.
- **Demographic detail:** Fresno LAFCo's own DUC narrative describes the park as 62 mobile homes and 5 single-family homes; CRLA's characterization of the residents as mostly Indigenous Mexican farmworker families is the source for the labor-reliance claim.

## Part of a larger pattern

Shady Lakes is the one case in this Fresno County pass where litigation produced a confirmed, dollar-figure outcome rather than an open question — a useful contrast with [Corcoran's](kings-corcoran.md) still-unresolved lawsuit in Kings County and the regional [1,2,3-TCP litigation](tulare-earlimart.md) that took years to reach settlement. It's also a reminder that this project's record includes housing-conditions litigation as well as contamination litigation — not every water-adjacent court case is about a well.
