# A state-named "Nitrate Relief Project" — Plainview, Tulare County

Plainview's nitrate problem is documented almost entirely through the state's own paperwork — a formally named "Plainview Nitrate Relief Project" working its way through California's environmental-review and water-board process.

## What happened

The Plainview Nitrate Relief Project moved through California's environmental-review process starting in 2023, under a Notice of Determination and an initial study filed with the State Water Resources Control Board. A separate public notice regarding Plainview's drinking water system appears in the state's 2026 records, and a notice of cancellation also appears for Plainview in the same filing year — details we could not fully reconcile in this pass; it's unclear from the documents reviewed whether the cancellation refers to the relief project itself or to a separate, unrelated filing.

## Impact — what we know, and what we don't

- **What, specifically, was cancelled in the 2026 filing:** Not resolved in this pass — the document title references cancellation, but the content wasn't retrievable in enough detail to confirm what it cancelled.
- **Population and farm-labor share:** Not captured in this pass.
- **Current nitrate levels:** Not established.

## Part of a larger pattern

Plainview is the thinnest-sourced case in this Tulare County pass, included deliberately as an honest example of a water problem that's documented in state paperwork but not yet in independent reporting — a contrast with the richly sourced [Earlimart](tulare-earlimart.md) and [Tooleville](tulare-tooleville.md) cases, and a reminder that this project's record is only as complete as the public sourcing allows.
