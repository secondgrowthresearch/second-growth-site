# Hershey closes its Oakdale candy plant and moves to Mexico — Stanislaus County, 2007–08

Hershey closed its Oakdale candy plant as part of a company-wide restructuring announced in 2007, shifting production to a new facility in Monterrey, Mexico and ending more than four decades of chocolate-making in the Stanislaus County town — one of at least three food and confectionery-sector disruptions the county absorbed within about a year, alongside the Signature Fruit Co. ownership change in Modesto.

## What happened

Hershey's Oakdale plant opened in 1965. In 2007, as part of a broader restructuring that cut about 1,500 jobs — roughly 11% of the company's total U.S. workforce — Hershey announced it would close some U.S. plants and shift production to a new facility under construction in Monterrey, Mexico. Production at Oakdale ceased in January 2008, per Hershey's own SEC filing. Oakdale's decades-old chocolate festival, tied to the plant's presence in town, was cancelled for good after the closure, ending a 30-year tradition.

Sconza Chocolates, relocating from Oakland, bought the Oakdale site in 2008 and committed to hiring former Hershey employees — some stayed on for years afterward. Sconza now employs 250 people at the plant.

## Impact — what we know, and what we don't

- **Jobs:** approximately 575 at Oakdale specifically, per a 2026 Modesto Bee retrospective; the broader 1,500-job figure is company-wide, not Oakdale-specific, and the Oakdale number hasn't been independently corroborated beyond that one source.
- **Closure date:** sources split between the 2007 announcement/restructuring year and the January 2008 production-end date in Hershey's own 10-K; both are cited here rather than resolved to one.
- **What became of the site:** fully resolved — Sconza Chocolates bought it in 2008 and still operates there.

## Part of a larger pattern

This is another instance of a pattern that shows up elsewhere in this project — a plant closes under its original owner, then reopens at a smaller scale under a new one — alongside [Foster Farms Turlock/Diestel Family Ranch](stanislaus-2025-foster-farms-turlock.md) and the Saputo-to-Marquez Brothers building sale documented in [Kraft's Louis Rich plant](../case-studies/tulare-1992-kraft-louis-rich-tulare.md) research. It also lands in the same narrow 2006–2007 window as [Seneca Foods' later purchase of Signature Fruit Co.'s Modesto operation](stanislaus-2018-seneca-foods-modesto.md) — meaning Stanislaus County absorbed at least three separate food-sector disruptions within about a year, years before the county's other documented clusters in 2012–14 and 2024–2026.
