# Olam's Lemoore tomato plant closes — Kings County, 2020

Olam West Coast, Inc. permanently closed its large tomato-processing plant in Lemoore, eliminating 567 jobs — the earliest of the closures this project tracks, and the one that first put a second Kings County town, not just Hanford, into this record.

## What happened

Olam announced in November 2019 that it would exit industrial tomato processing at Lemoore and scale back retail operations there, as part of a wider pullback that also closed its Woodland and Williams facilities the same year. A company representative told Tomato News in March 2020 that the Lemoore plant — about 250 employees at the time — would not operate that season and would be permanently shut by October. The state WARN filing, dated April 29, 2020, lists 567 employees affected, effective July 1, 2020 — a larger number than the ~250 reported working the plant months earlier, which this archive has not reconciled (it may reflect total plant headcount across shifts and seasons, not a single point-in-time count; see Open questions).

Industry reporting at the time cited rising grower costs, a tomato surplus depressing prices, a strong dollar hurting export competitiveness, and a trade dispute with the European Union — structural pressure on the sector, not a Lemoore-specific problem.

## Impact — what we know, and what we don't

- **Jobs:** 567 WARN-covered positions eliminated, effective July 1, 2020.
- **The site since:** As of February 2022 — the most recent source this archive has found — Olam was still trying to sell the property, splitting a 15-acre tomato-processing parcel for sale while keeping 10 acres for its separate granulated-garlic business. Lemoore's city manager called regaining those jobs "a major priority for the community" and said several buyers were interested.
- **What happened to the workers:** Not documented in any source found. No reemployment data, no union or workforce-agency accounting located.
- **Open question:** Whether a sale of the tomato-processing parcel ever closed, and whether any of the jobs came back, is unconfirmed past February 2022 — worth a direct check with the Kings County Economic Development Corporation or the City of Lemoore.

## Part of a larger pattern

This was the first of what this archive now documents as three separate commodity-processing closures landing on the same pair of Kings County towns, Hanford and Lemoore — this one, [Del Monte's Hanford tomato plant closure](kings-2024-del-monte-hanford.md) in 2024, and [Leprino Foods' Lemoore East dairy plant closure](kings-2025-leprino-lemoore.md) in 2025–26. A local news report on the Leprino closure noted in passing that Lemoore "has already lost a tomato plant and a grain mill" — the tomato plant is this closure; the grain mill remains an unconfirmed lead.
