# Data

Every number we publish is meant to be traceable back to a named, public source — not a proprietary dataset or an unsourced estimate. This page lists what we draw from.

## Employment and wages

**U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW).** County-level food-manufacturing (NAICS 311) employment and wages for Kings, Fresno, Tulare, and Stanislaus counties, 1990–2026, alongside matching California-statewide and U.S.-national benchmark data. The backbone of the [Sector Dependence Index](the-index.md).

## Trade

**U.S. Census Bureau, state exports and imports by NAICS code.** Monthly, state-level, international trade data for California's food-manufacturing sector, 2010–present — the basis for the real trade adjustment described in [Methodology](methodology.md).

## Output

**U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, Regional Economic Accounts (SAGDP2).** State-level GDP by industry, used as the output denominator for the trade adjustment.

## Closures and layoffs

**California Employment Development Department, WARN Act filings** — the formal notices employers are required to file ahead of mass layoffs, 2014–present. Paired with original news reporting and, where available, company records, for the specific closures in our [case studies](case-studies.md).

## Land and water

**California Department of Water Resources / Land IQ, Statewide Crop Mapping.** Field-level crop and idle/fallow land classification, 2014–2024. **DWR SGMA groundwater level measurements** for the Kings and Tulare Lake subbasins. Used to document the structural land- and water-use pressure behind the closures we study.

## Agricultural statistics

**USDA Census of Agriculture** (county-level, every five years, 1974–2022) and **county Agricultural Commissioner annual crop reports**, for the longest available run of farm counts, acreage, and labor figures in each county we track.

## Historical record

Primary and contemporaneous sourcing for Central Valley/Sacramento commodity-ag processing events going back to 1912 — cannery foundings, the 1966–67 United Farm Workers ownership precedent at Delano's Forty Acres, and documented plant closures from the early 1980s forward — used to test whether the current wave of closures is part of a genuinely continuous, decades-long pattern rather than a recent, isolated event. See [Case Studies](case-studies.md) for the timeline built from this record.

## Photographs

Historical photographs on this site are drawn from the Library of Congress's Farm Security Administration/Office of War Information Collection and Historic American Buildings Survey — U.S. government works, confirmed public domain on a per-item basis before use, not assumed from a collection's general reputation. Several other candidate images (1966 Delano UFW photographs, Sacramento cannery interior photographs) were identified and explicitly excluded after turning out to be under copyright.

## A note on completeness

Several of the sources above have real, documented gaps — some county crop reports are missing for specific years, some historical events are more thinly sourced than others. We'd rather name a gap than imply completeness we don't have. As our methodology page notes, this is a working record, not a finished one, and we update it as the gaps close.
